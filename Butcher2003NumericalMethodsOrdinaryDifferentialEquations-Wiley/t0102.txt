having a specific vertex designated as the root. The ‘order’ of a tree is the
number of vertices in this tree. If the order is greater than one, then the
‘leaves’ of a tree are the vertices from which there are no outward-growing
arcs; in other words, a leaf is a vertex, other than the root, which has exactly
one other vertex joined to it.

An assortment of trees of various orders, with leaves and the root indicated
in each case, is shown in Figure 234(i). In pictorial representations of particular
rooted trees, as in this Figure, we use a convention of placing the root at the
lowest point in the picture.

For each tree t, a corresponding polynomial in the coefficients of the method
can be written down. Denote this by @(£). Also, associated with each tree t is
an integer +(t). We now explain how ©(¢) and 7(é) are constructed.

In the case of &(t), associate with each vertex of the tree, except the leaves,
a label i, 7, ..., and assume that 7 is the label attached to the root. Write down
a sequence of factors of which the first is ;. For each arc of the tree, other
than an arc that terminates in a leaf, write down a factor, say aj,, where j
and k are the beginning and end of the arc (assuming that all directions are in
the sense of movement away from the root). Finally, for each arc terminating
at a leaf, write down a factor, say cj, where j is the label attached to the
beginning of this arc. Having written down this sequence of factors, sum their
product for all possible choices of each of the labels, in the set {1,2,...,s}.

To find the value of +(£), associate a factor with each vertex of the tree. For
the leaves this factor is 1, and for all other vertices it is equal to 1, added to
the factors attached to all outwards growing neighbours. The product of the
factors, for all vertices of the tree, is the value of y(t).
