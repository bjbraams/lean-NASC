In Butcher and Chipman (1992), the search for possible » corresponding
to A-stable methods was focused on the cases 2v) — p € {0,1,2}. For k = 1,
that is the one-step case, this is necessary and sufficient for A-stability.
It seems to be the case that, even for k > 1, those methods for which
2v9 — p > 2 cannot be A-stable. This proposition, which has become known
as the ‘Butcher-Chipman conjecture’, has been proved in Butcher (2002) for
the cases 2v) — p = 3,4,7,8,11,12,....

If the conjecture can be proved in general, it is likely that order stars, or
some very similar technique, will play a role in the proof. Because examples
exist, namely the cases [23, 0, 2] and [24, 0, 2], for which poles lie in unbounded
fingers, G. Wanner has pointed out that the use of order stars in such a proof,
will run into complications. On the other hand, it is possible that order arrows
might provide an alternative approach. Order arrows for these two apparently
difficult cases are shown in Figure 521(i).
