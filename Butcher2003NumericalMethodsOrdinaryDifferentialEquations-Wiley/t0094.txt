This method is an example of a ‘linear multistep method’.

Before we can carry out numerical tests with this method, we first need
some procedure for carrying out the first step of the computation. Once 4;
is calculated, the information that is needed for the computation of y2, and
subsequently the solution at later steps, will be available as they are needed. In
the experiments we report here, the first step is taken using the Runge-Kutta
method introduced in the previous subsection.

The errors are shown in Table 222(I) and we see that, for this problem
at least, the results are just as good as for the Runge-Kutta method (221a)
and (221b), even though only one derivative is computed in each step. In
fact, for components number 1 and 4, better than second order convergence
is observed.
