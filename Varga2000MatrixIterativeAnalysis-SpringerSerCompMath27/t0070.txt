Theorem 2.25 of Boyle and Hendelman, an inverse eigenvalue problem,
is of course interesting for numerical analysts. Because of the possibly
large number of zero eigenvalues (n — m) of the primitive n x n matrix
A, having the m-tuple A as its non-zero spectrum, it would seem that
an algorithm for computationally finding the matrix A would have
difficulties with this large null space!

The use of directed graphs in matrix theory is not new, but recently
several authors (cf. Harary (1959) and Parter (1961a)) have applied
their geometrical aspects to the classical problems of Gaussian elimi-
nation and the determination of eigenvalues of large matrices. Specific
use of directed graphs will be made in Chap. 6 in analyzing the matrix
properties of matrix equations, derived from elliptic (and parabolic)
partial differential equations.
