An entry is **covered** when its mathematical claims appear as proved results,
including direct instances and equivalent representations. It is **partly
covered** when a proved result matches a claim within the entry or a special
case of it. **Not covered** means the review identified no such result.
Definitions, analogous estimates, and results that still require an argument
to yield the claimed numerical conclusion are recorded as ingredients. In
particular, a general approximation framework contributes coverage only to
the specific assertions it supplies.

The two libraries usually index ordered spectra from zero. A finite matrix
can be represented by its linear map on Euclidean space, with its induced
operator norm, or by its entries, with a different norm. The links below
identify which interpretation is used when it affects the comparison.

## 1. Floating-point arithmetic and rounding error analysis

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS.md#1-floating-point-arithmetic-and-rounding-error-analysis).

### Mathlib

The module `Mathlib.Data.FP.Basic` defines a radix-2 floating-point type with a validity predicate on finite encodings and conversion to rationals, for a fixed precision and maximal exponent. The whole module is deprecated (since 2026-10-02), its arithmetic is implemented by `unsafe` definitions with no correctness theorems, and it has no rounding functions on the reals, so it supplies none of the claims of 1.1–1.5; a format-independent real-valued model is needed instead. [`ValidFinite`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/Data/FP/Basic.lean#L65), [`toRat`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/Data/FP/Basic.lean#L81)

[`Mathlib/Tactic/Inclusion/Extension/IntervalDyadicReal/Init.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/Tactic/Inclusion/Extension/IntervalDyadicReal/Init.lean)

## 3. Direct methods for linear systems and least squares

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS.md#3-direct-methods-for-linear-systems-and-least-squares).

### Mathlib

Positive matrices have an LDL factorization in terms of Gram–Schmidt, and projection onto a complete inner-product subspace gives the closest vector. These are the basic exact-arithmetic constructions for Cholesky and least squares. Matrix inversion, determinant products and the block elimination identities of the Schur complement support LU, and the determinant of a triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal. Echelon decomposition certificates record a row permutation and a lower triangular matrix with nonzero diagonal that bring a matrix to echelon form; they are a carrier for $PA=LU$ but do not bound the multipliers. [`det_of_isUpperTriangular`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Block.lean#L403), [`det_fromBlocks₁₁`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/SchurComplement.lean#L368), [`Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Echelon/Decomposition.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Echelon/Decomposition.lean), [`Mathlib/Analysis/Matrix/LDL.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/Analysis/Matrix/LDL.lean), [`Mathlib/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Projection/Basic.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Projection/Basic.lean), [`Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/SchurComplement.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/SchurComplement.lean)

## 4. Eigenvalue and singular value computations

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS.md#4-eigenvalue-and-singular-value-computations).

### Mathlib

For 4.2, the spectral theorem provides unitary diagonalization with real eigenvalues for Hermitian matrices, including real symmetric matrices via the real instance. For 4.3, the singular values of a finite-dimensional linear map are the ordered square roots of the eigenvalues of the adjoint product, with their positive support equal to the rank. The Hessenberg API records characteristic-polynomial preservation under a similarity certificate and implements algebraic reductions for concrete matrices. It supplies ingredients for 4.8, which specifies a unitary reduction and shifted QR iteration. [`Matrix.IsHermitian.spectral_theorem`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/Analysis/Matrix/Spectrum.lean#L143), [`LinearMap.hasEigenvalue_adjoint_comp_self_sq_singularValues`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/SingularValues.lean#L137), [`LinearMap.card_support_singularValues`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/SingularValues.lean#L174), [`Matrix.Hessenberg.Similarity.charpoly_eq`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/Hessenberg/Similarity.lean#L53), [`Mathlib/Tactic/Hessenberg/Reduce.lean`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/fc4f59e577feb1a4956671bc04ac18aaaf8cd842/Mathlib/Tactic/Hessenberg/Reduce.lean)

### TauCeti

For 4.2, a normal complex matrix is diagonalized by a unitary matrix, and the algebraic and geometric multiplicities of its eigenvalues agree. The converse implications and the Frobenius-norm criterion are not stated. [`Matrix.exists_mem_unitaryGroup_star_mul_mul_eq_diagonal`](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/d91980bdc255e6e50506be147ad9bcb88eb9fcc0/TauCeti/Analysis/Matrix/Normal.lean#L91), [`Matrix.rootMultiplicity_charpoly_of_isStarNormal`](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCeti/blob/d91980bdc255e6e50506be147ad9bcb88eb9fcc0/TauCeti/Analysis/Matrix/Normal.lean#L100)

For 4.3, there is a unitary SVD for square matrices with the singular values in decreasing order on the diagonal, an orthonormal rank-one expansion for linear maps between finite-dimensional inner product spaces, and the Frobenius seminorm as the root of the sum of squared singular values. The Hermitian dilation and its eigenvectors are available on a product of inner-product spaces. ## 24. High-dimensional approximation, uncertainty quantification, and model reduction

[Mathematical scope](SYNOPSIS.md#24-high-dimensional-approximation-uncertainty-quantification-and-model-reduction).

### Mathlib

For 24.13, Gaussian measures on real normed spaces are defined by linear images, and Fernique's exponential integrability theorem applies in the separable Banach setting. Gaussian measures on Euclidean spaces are available with prescribed covariance, including degenerate cases through the positive semidefinite matrix interface. ## Principal gaps

- **Finite-precision contract and verified computation.** The arithmetic
  model in §1 and exact interval operations in §7 are common prerequisites
  for error-free transforms, residual tests, and backward-error proofs for
  factorizations and iterative refinement in §§2–3.
- **Exact numerical linear algebra.** The spectral and projection results
  in §§2–6 give a basis for CG and GMRES, perturbation of singular subspaces,
  and orthogonality and residual relations for Krylov eigenvalue methods.
  Several of these targets require only finite-dimensional exact arithmetic.
- **Approximation and quadrature.** The interpolation and orthogonality
  APIs provide starting points for the Lagrange remainder, positive
  quadrature, and the Jacobi-matrix treatment of Gauss nodes and weights
  in §§10–12. In §13, finite character orthogonality and the DFT can support
  cyclic convolution, Fourier-matrix diagonalization, and FFT correctness.
- **Differential and integral discretizations.** Abstract continuous
  well-posedness in §§14, 18–19, and 21 can be reused once the discrete
  operators and comparison maps are in place. These include time-stepping
  schemes, grids and finite-element spaces, projections for second-kind
  equations, and the ingredients of adaptive convergence.
- **Inverse problems and stochastic approximation.** The compact spectral
  and probability APIs support regularization, randomized integration,
  Gaussian-series methods, and numerical SDE analysis. The error and
  complexity statements depend on the corresponding estimators, schemes,
  and regularization or discretization parameters in §§22–24.
