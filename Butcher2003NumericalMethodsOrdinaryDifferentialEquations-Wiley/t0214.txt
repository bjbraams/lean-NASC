If the sign of the coefficients are then all reversed, the resulting method is
known as the ‘reflection’ (Scherer, 1977, 1978) of the original method. Because
the exact solution is its own reflection, it is natural to consider whether
Runge-Kutta methods that have this property have any advantage over other
methods. In particular, the Gauss methods are their own reflections, as we
will see. Reflected methods are now commonly known as ‘adjoint methods’;
for references to modern applications and research see Hairer, Lubich and
Wanner (2002).
