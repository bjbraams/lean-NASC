It was known for some time that if the matrix A is real, symmetric and
positive definite, then either the point or block Gauss-Seidel iterative
method is convergent (von Mises and Pollaczek-Geiringer (1929)). The
converse was first established by Reich (1949) and later by Geiringer
(1949) and Schmeidler (1949). Theorem 3.12, which includes these
results as the special case w = 1, is a less general form of the result by
Ostrowski (1954). See also Householder (1958) and Weissinger (1951)
and Weissinger (1953). The proof we have given for Theorem 3.12
is based on Ostrowski (1954)’s use of quadratic forms, but different
proofs are given by Householder (1958), who uses basic properties of
matrix norms, and Reich (1949). For extensions to nearly symmetric
matrices, see Stein (1951) and Ostrowski (1954).

There is an interesting extension of the Ostrowski-Reich Theorem 3.12
by Kuznetsov (1984), mentioned in Exer. 5 of Section 3.4, which also
extends to the cases when the real symmetric matrix A is positive-
semidefinite or negative-semidefinite. The Ostrowski-Reich Theorem
3.12 and Kuznetsov’s result both use quadratic form arguments in
their proofs.
