The Gerschgorin Circle Theorem 1.11, by Gerschgorin (1931), and
its sharpening, via irreducibility by Taussky (1948) in Theorem 1.18,
are beautiful and easily applied results which have continued to fas-
cinate researchers in linear algebra. As a consequence, there is an
ever-growing large body of newer development in this area, stem-
ming from these two seminal papers. This includes extensions of Ger-
schgorin’s Theorem 1.11 to partitioned matrices (Feingold and Varga
(1962), Robert (1964) and Salas (1999)), to minimal Gerschgorin sets
(Varga (1965), Levinger and Varga (1966), Varga (1970), Carlson and
Varga (1973a), Carlson and Varga (1973b), and Krautstengl and Varga
(1995)), and to G-functions (Hoffman (1969), Hoffman (1971), Hoff-
man (1975/6) and Nowosad (1965)).

The definition of the matrix property of irreducibility is due to Frobe-
nius (1912). The equivalent formulation of irreducibility as given in
Exer. 4 is due to Geiringer (1949). Its use in Theorem 1.18 to sharpen
Gerschgorin’s Theorem (1.10) is due to Taussky (1948). See also
Taussky (1962).

For introductory material on finite directed graphs, see Kénig (1950)
and Berge (1958). There also arise some new computational problems
of determining when a given large square matrix A is irreducible. For
applications of graph theoretic arguments to matrix theory, see Harary
(1959) and Harary (1960). Of course, all these concepts of irreducible
and reducible nonnegative matrices, directed graphs etc., have connec-
tions with the theory of finite Markov chains. See Kemeny and Snell
(1960).

That an n x n strictly diagonally dominant matrix is nonsingular is an
old result which can be traced back at least to Lévy (1881) and Des-
planques (1887), and this result is often called the Lévy-Desplanques
Theorem (see Horn and Johnson (1985), p. 349). It has been repeat-
edly rediscovered in the literature! It is also interesting to note that
this theorem is actually equivalent to Gerschgorin’s Theorem 1.11. (See
Exer. 6 of this section.) It is similarly the case that any n x n irre-
ducibly diagonally dominant matrix being nonsingular is equivalent to
Taussky’s Theorem 1.18. (See Exer. 7 of this section.)
