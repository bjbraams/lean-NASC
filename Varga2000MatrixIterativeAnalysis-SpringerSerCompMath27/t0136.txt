Finally, in the numerical solution by iterative methods of practical prob-
lems, one generally is not given, nor can one precisely determine a priori,
the spectral radius of the block Jacobi or the block Gauss-Seidel iteration
matrices. Thus, in practice we would determine estimates of the optimum re-
laxation factor wy, of (4.29) or (4.30), from estimates of p(B). In this regard,
simple monotonicity arguments applied to (4.29) show that overestimates and
underestimates of p(B) give, respectively, overestimates and underestimates
of w». But, from the nature of the determination of w, by the coalescing of
two eigenvalues, it was originally shown by Young (1954a) that overestimat-
ing p(B) by a small amount causes a smaller decrease in R.o(L,,) than does
underestimating p(B) by a comparable amount. This is best seen for the case
p = 2 by plotting p(L.,) as a function of w for 0 <w < 2.
